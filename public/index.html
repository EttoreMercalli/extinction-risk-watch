Scientific Methodology & Transparency

Why this calculation is grounded and defensible: the theoretical bases of evolutionary genetics and anthropogenic risk estimates.

🧬 The Natural Biological Expiration Date (~200,000 Years)

From the standpoint of population genetics and evolutionary paleontology, a mammalian species like Homo sapiens has an average lifespan of approximately 1–2 million years. Having emerged roughly 200,000–300,000 years ago, the remaining time before natural genetic decay or speciation is estimated at around 200,000 years (~73 million days). This is humanity's built-in "expiration date" — and everything we do as a civilization is either preserving or shortening it.

Key References:
  • Science (2023): Genomic inference of a severe human bottleneck during the Early to Middle Pleistocene transition (Hu, Hao, Du, et al.).
  • Nature (1993): Implications of the Copernican Principle on our Future Prospects (J. Richard Gott III, Princeton University).

⚠️ Anthropogenic Compression in the 21st Century

Over the past century, humanity has developed technological capabilities with the potential for total self-destruction — nuclear weapons, engineered synthetic biology, misaligned artificial intelligence, and accelerating climate crisis. According to the Future of Humanity Institute at Oxford University, the risk of extinction within the 21st century alone is approximately 1 in 6 (16%) — comparable to a single roll of a die.
